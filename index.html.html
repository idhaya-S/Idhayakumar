Amphiphiles in, architecture out.

My Ph.D. in Biotechnology at Ramaiah Institute of Technology links biosurfactant micelle self-assembly to pore architecture, surface charge and light-driven function in TiO₂ nanoparticles.

I quantify the interfacial interactions with kinetic, isotherm and thermodynamic models, and I write the Python tools to fit them.

Next, I want to bring self-assembly and surface-chemistry expertise to light-responsive nanodevices.

One molecule, two behaviours

Saponin biosurfactants are amphiphilic metabolites: a water-loving head and a water-avoiding tail. In solution they self-assemble into micelles, heads outside and tails packed inside.

That micelle is the soft template. Its geometry sets the pore dimensions and surface properties of the TiO₂ built around it.

Rhamnolipids from Pseudomonas and Enterococcus

Rhamnolipids are glycolipid biosurfactants: a rhamnose sugar head on a fatty-acid tail. I work mostly with bacterial rhamnolipids from Pseudomonas and Enterococcus. Like saponins, they are amphiphilic and self-assemble into micelles.

How a biosurfactant shapes a nanoparticle

Thesis: biosurfactant-mediated mesoporous TiO₂ nanoparticles. The amphiphilic saponin micelle is the template, so its geometry sets pore dimensions and surface properties. Dye and metal removal is one of the ways I test the result.

Nanomaterials meet biology

Antimicrobial activity of copper nanomaterials is the subject of my 2022 book chapter. At CSIR-NIO, Goa, I compared culturable nitrate-reducing bacteria in the Arabian Sea oxygen minimum zone and non-OMZ regions. In a TNSCST-funded project I standardised Spirulina maxima supplementation for banana micropropagation.

The biological methods include fluorescence microscopy, PCR, fluorometry, UV spectrophotometry and sequencing data analysis with ODV, PASTA, NCBI, BLAST and SPSS.

Quantifying the interface

I fit pseudo-first and pseudo-second-order kinetics, intraparticle diffusion, four isotherm models and thermodynamic parameters to extract binding mechanism and rate-limiting steps. I built a Python/Streamlit suite to model how molecules interact with TiO₂ nanoparticles.
